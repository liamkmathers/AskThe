# Vision

## North Star

> **Every fan question gets the creator's own answer, from the exact moment they said it.**

## The problem

Niche creators build deep expertise over hundreds of videos: which jacket is warmest, which boots last, which denim is worth the price. That knowledge is locked in hours of video. Only dedicated fans who have watched everything can act on it. Everyone else gets a fraction, or asks the same question in the comments that was answered two years ago.

## The idea

Ingest every video of a creator into a structured knowledge base of their claims, verdicts and recommendations. Fans ask in plain language and get an answer that:

- comes only from what the creator actually said,
- links to the exact moment in the video where they said it,
- reflects the creator's latest opinion, and says so when they changed their mind,
- admits when the creator hasn't covered something.

## The template: "Ask The ___"

AskThe is one engine with many instances. Each creator's instance is named after them (**Ask The Iron Snail**) and framed around their subject: product categories, vocabulary, the kinds of questions their fans ask. The engine itself never assumes a particular creator or niche. A new creator is a new config file and a new ingest run, not a rebuild.

**First instance:** The Iron Snail, a men's clothing review channel (about 670k subscribers) covering jackets, denim, boots and other gear. Its buyer's-guide style content makes it an ideal first case.

## Who it serves

Three groups have to win, or the product doesn't work.

| Who | What they want | What they get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fans and buyers | A straight answer before buying | The creator's verdict, the reasoning, and a link to the clip |
| The creator | More views, more income, less repetition | Traffic sent back to videos, affiliate revenue, insight into what fans ask |
| Us | A repeatable business | Revenue share, and a pipeline we can reuse for the next creator |

**Example fan questions (Iron Snail):**

- "What's the warmest jacket he's reviewed under $300?"
- "Which boots does he recommend for wet winters, and which ones did he say fell apart?"
- "Is raw denim from brand X worth it compared to brand Y?"
- "How do I break in leather boots the way he does it?"
- "Did he change his mind about this jacket after the long-term review?"

**Example creator questions (future dashboard):**

- What are fans asking most this month?
- Which questions have no good answer in my videos yet? (video ideas)
- Which old videos get the most traffic from the assistant?

## Principles

These don't bend. Every design and product decision is checked against them.

1. **Knowledge over personality.** Accuracy and sources come first. We build a knowledge expert, not a clone. Tone or speaking style is a later, optional layer, if ever.
2. **Grounded or silent.** Every answer cites the videos it comes from. If the creator never covered something, the assistant says so instead of guessing.
3. **Send people back to the videos.** Timestamp links are a core feature. The assistant grows the creator's views rather than replacing them.
4. **Time-aware opinions.** Creators change their minds. The newest verdict wins, and changes of opinion are shown openly.
5. **Built with the creator, never around them.** Nothing goes public without the creator's agreement. Their content, their name, their final say.

## Positioning

Clone platforms (such as Delphi) sell a general "digital you" to the creator, and audiences have reacted badly to some of them. Fans want the creator's answers, not an imitation of the creator.

We sell a **structured product-and-advice knowledge base for a specific niche**, paid for by the purchases it helps fans make. We win on niche depth, product structure, and a revenue share that makes saying yes easy.

## Business model (for later phases)

The prototype is private, unmonetised and for testing only. The model below is where we're headed once a creator is on board.

| Stream | How it works | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affiliate revenue share | Answers link to recommended products; commission split with the creator | From the first pilot |
| Fan membership tier | Unlimited or advanced questions as a paid perk, e.g. via the creator's membership | After pilot, if fans engage |
| Creator insights | Dashboard of fan questions and content gaps, possibly a paid add-on | After pilot |
| Vertical knowledge product | Several creators in one niche answering together | Later, each creator opted in |

Gear and clothing is the right start because almost every question carries buying intent. Niches without products to buy would need a membership model instead.
